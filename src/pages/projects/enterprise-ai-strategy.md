## The problem

Alter Domus was exploring how to use artificial intelligence across the business, but the real question was where AI could actually improve the way work was being done.

I worked with the Director of Client Portals and the Director of Artificial Intelligence to identify practical opportunities across client onboarding and data workflows. The goal was to give leadership a clear view of where AI could reduce manual work, improve efficiency, and support growth.

## Design constraints

*The proposal had to solve real business problems.* We focused on actual workflows, not AI for the sake of AI. Each use case needed a clear purpose.

*Automation also creates risk.* We considered where human review still mattered, what controls were needed, and what could go wrong if a process was automated.

*The ideas had to scale.* We looked for opportunities that could work across teams and clients rather than solving a problem for only one workflow.

*The audience was senior leadership.* The Steering Committee needed the business value, risks, and implementation approach explained clearly without unnecessary technical detail.

## What I did

I helped build the enterprise AI strategy from the business and implementation side.

We reviewed client onboarding and data workflows, identified repetitive work that could potentially be automated, and looked at where AI could make processes faster or easier to scale.

For each opportunity, we asked what problem we were solving, where people still needed to be involved, what controls were necessary, and how the solution could be implemented across the business.

We turned those ideas into an executive-ready roadmap covering use cases, governance, and implementation, then presented it to Alter Domus’ Steering Committee.

## Outcome

We delivered an AI business case and roadmap for Steering Committee review.

The proposal gave leadership a structured way to evaluate AI opportunities instead of treating AI as one large technology initiative. It showed where automation could create value, where controls were necessary, and how the company could move from ideas into implementation.

For me, the biggest lesson was that an AI strategy is really a process strategy. You first have to understand the work, what is slowing people down, and what should or should not change. Then you decide whether AI is the right tool.

## Takeaways

- **Start with the process, not the technology.** If I cannot explain the business problem without mentioning AI, I probably do not understand the problem well enough yet.
- **Not everything that can be automated should be automated.** A good AI strategy also identifies where people, approvals, and controls still need to exist.
- **Leadership needs a business case, not an AI lesson.** Senior leaders need to understand the opportunity, risk, cost, and path forward. The technical details only matter when they help answer those questions.
- **Scalability has to be considered early.** A solution that saves one employee ten minutes is useful. A process that can be repeated across teams, clients, and workflows is much more valuable.
- **Strategy still has to lead to execution.** I do not think a roadmap is useful if nobody can explain what the first few steps would actually look like. Our proposal included both the opportunities and an approach for putting them into practice.
